The project

From brain scans to better research questions.

A brain scan can show where the brain is changing. Our question is what those changes mean, and what researchers should measure next.

I’m Ricardo Félix Morais. Together with my colleagues Alex Chen and Seika Karamatsu, I’m building DopaTeam to make that next research decision easier to examine.

What is the idea?

Brain cells communicate through chemical signals called neurotransmitters. Reference maps show where parts of these chemical systems are usually found in the brain.

Comparing these maps with changes seen on brain scans can point researchers towards a possible explanation. But a similar pattern does not prove that a chemical system is causing the problem.

DopaTeam starts with that uncertainty. It lets us compare possible explanations and ask what extra evidence would tell them apart.

The goal is simple: give researchers a clearer reason to choose one experiment over another.

What have we already done?

  1. Built on published neuroimaging research.

    I am the first author of a 2025 study in Neurobiology of Disease examining brain structure alongside neurotransmitter reference maps in Alzheimer’s and related conditions. Separately, we reviewed earlier research code on neurotransmitters and brain cell types to understand its methods and limits.

    Read the published study

  2. Built a working pattern comparison.

    Our starting code compares an invented brain pattern across 82 illustrative regions with 30 synthetic reference maps. It ranks the matches and checks how much they change when a region is left out.

  3. Built a demonstration with two possible explanations.

    The same imaging inputs are used in two simulated scenarios. One preserves useful function. The other loses it. We compare additional measurements to show which one could reveal the difference.

  4. Checked that the calculations work.

    The prototype has numerical tests and an independent Python calculation. The initial comparison module also passes eight tests. These checks support a working demonstration; testing its value in real research is the next step.

The full prototype runs locally. This website explains the work. The demonstration uses invented values, not patient results.

A simple example.

Two brain circuits could look similar in the available scan, yet be doing different things.

One is coping.

Cells work harder and keep the circuit’s output at a useful level.

One is struggling.

The circuit no longer maintains that useful output.

In our example, another score from the same scan leaves the question open. An independent measure of function can separate the two scenarios. If that measurement is too noisy, the distinction becomes harder.

This gives us a concrete next question: which functional measurement would be useful in a real biological experiment?

How could this help drug research?

A change in brain activity might be helping the brain cope, or it might reflect a failing system. Those explanations could lead researchers to test different treatments.

Our ambition is to help researchers examine that choice before testing a drug. We have not discovered a treatment or shown that DopaTeam improves drug development. We have built a small, testable starting point.
