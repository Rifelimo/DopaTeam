There are treatments. We still need much better ones.

Medicines such as donepezil and memantine help manage symptoms. Newer treatments, including lecanemab and donanemab, can slow decline in selected people with early Alzheimer’s.

They do not cure the disease or restore abilities already lost. The newer treatments also require careful patient selection and monitoring because serious side effects can occur, including brain swelling and bleeding.

The opportunity is to find treatments that preserve useful function for longer, reach more people and are safer to use.

What does the market represent?

The cost of care shows how much is at stake for families, health systems and payers. It is much broader than spending on medicines.

There is already a treatment business. Leqembi, the brand name for lecanemab, recorded US$184 million in global sales in the second quarter of 2026, including US$97 million in the United States. That is one medicine in one quarter, not the size of the whole Alzheimer’s market.

Biogen, second quarter 2026 results

For DopaTeam, the proposed first customers are research groups and biotechnology companies choosing which experiments to fund. The commercial question is whether our tool helps them make a better decision, and whether they would pay for that help.

We have not yet measured customer demand or set a price. The $409 billion care burden is not a revenue estimate for DopaTeam.

Why could our question matter for new drugs?

Before trying to change a biological process, researchers need to understand what it is doing. Is a change helping the brain maintain function, or does it reflect a system that is failing?

That distinction could influence which drug target deserves an experiment. Our proposed contribution is a clearer way to compare the explanations and identify the missing measurement. Better drug discovery is the longer term goal.

A short pitch you can use

The human reason to do this.

Alzheimer’s takes away ordinary things: a conversation, a familiar route, the ability to live independently. In the US, an estimated 7.4 million people aged 65 and older have Alzheimer’s dementia. Families and health systems carry an enormous burden.

We have treatments, but we still need much better ones. To develop them, we need to understand whether a change in the brain is helping it cope or showing that it is failing.

I’m Ricardo Félix Morais. With Alex and Seika, I’m building DopaTeam to help researchers examine that question and choose what to measure next. We have a working simulation, grounded in a question from published neuroimaging research. The next step is to test its value in a real research decision.

For families, the goal is more independence and more time together. Our work starts with a better reason for the next experiment.
